6. Paddle or Stripe

The big difference is tax. Paddle is the merchant of record: it is the seller on every receipt, so it collects and pays sales tax and VAT everywhere. With Stripe, VC Innovations Group is the seller and owes those taxes itself.

The tax work Paddle takes off our hands

  • European Union VAT, from the first sale. A US business selling software to people in the EU owes VAT from the first euro, with no minimum. It registers once (the non-Union OSS scheme) and files every quarter.
  • United Kingdom VAT, from the first sale, filed separately.
  • Other countries (Canada, Australia, Norway, Switzerland, Japan, and more) each have their own rules and minimums.
  • US sales tax. About half the states tax software subscriptions. A state's tax is owed once sales there pass its line, usually $100,000 a year (some still also count 200 sales; 16 states dropped that count by 2026). Texas taxes software subscriptions (on 80% of the price), and because we are based in Texas we would owe it on Texas buyers from the first sale.
  • With Paddle, all of this is Paddle's: it charges the right tax, files, and pays. We get one payout and one monthly statement.
Recommendation: Paddle. At our prices its fee is a bit higher than Stripe's on paper (see section 3), but Stripe leaves us registering for VAT on day one and filing in several places, which costs more in time and accountant fees than the difference. Lemon Squeezy, the other common choice, is being folded into Stripe, so we'd skip it. If the business grows large, moving to Stripe later is possible.
