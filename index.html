Groups and shared brains

One memory for a whole client, product, or team

Claude Code reads a CLAUDE.md file in each project folder. That covers one folder. Most work spans several: a website, an app, a folder of brand files, and the threads where decisions were made.

  • Put projects, threads, bots, and files in a group. Anything can be in more than one group.
  • Give the group a brain: one page with what it's about, what was decided, how the pieces connect, and what's happening now.
  • Every message you send and every bot run in the group starts with that page.
  • A new brain catches up on what the group already holds. After that, a keeper reads new commits, reports, and threads each day or week and suggests updates for you to approve.

Your desktop and your laptop

Start on your desktop, keep going on your laptop

Pair your laptop with your home PC once. Both screens show the same six digits, and you click Allow. After that, the laptop connects on its own whenever both PCs are on.

  • The laptop shows the home PC's threads, groups, bots, and runs as they happen.
  • Replies and bot runs happen on the home PC, with its files and its Claude Code. Nothing has to be copied or synced.
  • Messages between your PCs are encrypted end to end. Our server passes them along and can't read them.

Bots you can see

Build a bot by filling in a flow, not a config file

Each bot does one job, like reviewing new commits, checking that docs are current, or researching a topic each week. It's drawn as six steps: when it runs, where, its job, its rules, what it may use, and where the results go. Click a step to change it. The sentence at the top always says what the bot will do.

  • Start from a template, or describe the bot in a sentence.
  • Test run it and watch each step before it runs on its own.
  • Run it on a schedule, after each commit, or by hand. Schedules keep running with the app closed.
  • Bots can only read unless you allow more. They never commit, push, deploy, or publish.
